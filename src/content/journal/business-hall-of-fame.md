## From one conversation to a regional stage

On April 16, 2026, I delivered the student testimonial at Junior Achievement of OKI Partners’ Greater Butler & Warren Counties Business Hall of Fame. Standing at the Oscar Event Center in Fairfield, I had the opportunity to tell business and community leaders what their investment in students could make possible.

I was selected from more than **70,000 applicants** to deliver the student testimonial. It was an honor to bring a student perspective into an evening celebrating people who had spent decades building businesses and serving southwestern Ohio.

## It started with one conversation

About a year before the ceremony, I reached out to Junior Achievement with an idea: how could we give more students a chance to experience entrepreneurship for themselves? I connected with Steve Brokamp, and that first conversation grew into collaborations with educators, students, mentors, and business professionals.

> “I made a call, we had a conversation, and we decided to get started.”

That line from my speech captured how much of this journey began: with a willingness to ask, and people willing to help.

## Bringing opportunity into elementary classrooms

I led and coordinated more than 15 high school volunteers delivering Junior Achievement programming to third- and fourth-grade classrooms in Mason. Our pilot reached nearly **200 elementary students**, introducing financial literacy, career readiness, and entrepreneurship.

Working with teachers and Junior Achievement helped us bring these ideas into a familiar setting. I wanted students to have the chance to ask questions about their futures earlier—and to see older students as people they could learn from.

> “We saw students asking questions, thinking bigger, and beginning to believe in their own potential.”

The experience also shaped an ambition to build a broader K–12 pathway for entrepreneurship education.

## Helping students build their own ventures

As a cofounder of Mason Venture Studio, I helped create a place where students could move from an idea to the practical work of developing a venture. In the account I shared for this event, the initiative supported **60+ students and 15+ startups and venture ideas** across different stages of development.

Four teams participated in the Junior Achievement Social Innovation Challenge. My team, developing MindMate Health, advanced nationally through Junior Achievement Future Bound.

Alongside Junior Achievement and the Project Management Institute, we worked on an AI-focused adaptation of JA Innovation Generation. Steve Brokamp, Jeff Brokamp, our experiential learning advisor Joe Schroeder, Dr. Michael Lively, and Michelle Morrison were among the people supporting that work. We also presented the initiative and student projects at Summit 2025, in collaboration with PMI and the University of Cincinnati’s Lindner College of Business.

The value of those connections was practical: students could ask questions, get professional feedback, and explain their work beyond a classroom.

## Speaking to the people who built before us

The evening honored five laureates: **Catherine Evans, George Lang, Steve Lippert, Andy Schuster, and Sherry Ratcliffe Taylor**. Their names appear in the supplied event program below.

![The printed event program listing Varshith Ulisi’s student testimonial and the five inductees](/images/hall-of-fame/9094.webp)

I wanted my remarks to recognize their example and the support that had made our own initiatives possible.

> “You did not just find success, you built something lasting.”

My speech connected the elementary classroom pilot, Venture Studio, our work with PMI, and MindMate Health. The thread running through it was gratitude: these opportunities grew because people chose to believe in students while our ideas were still taking shape.

## The response I did not expect

After I spoke, **all five laureates acknowledged me by name during their acceptance remarks**, sharing encouragement about my speech and the student leadership I had described.

Hearing that encouragement during a night dedicated to their accomplishments meant a great deal to me. They had years of experience behind them, yet they made room in their own remarks for a student and the work of other young people.

## An evening I will carry forward

When I first contacted Junior Achievement, I was not imagining a Hall of Fame stage. I wanted to build something useful. The invitation gave me a chance to show where that work had led—and to thank the people who helped it happen.

The responsibility I took from that evening is to keep creating opportunities for others. The support I received should continue through the students I teach, the founders I work with, and the projects we build together.
