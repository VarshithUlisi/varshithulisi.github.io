On November 17, 2026, I represented **Mason Venture Studio at the Ohio School Boards Association Capital Conference**, bringing our student-led business incubator to an audience of around **40 superintendents and school board members**. It was an opportunity to show education leaders what students can build when a school gives them meaningful responsibility, practical support, and room to lead.

Our session, **“Empowering student entrepreneurs: Career readiness at Mason City,”** took place from 10:30 to 11:30 a.m. in Room A223 at the Greater Columbus Convention Center. Alongside fellow student leaders and our advisor, I shared how Mason Venture Studio fits into Mason’s broader Experiential Learning Program—and how we turn entrepreneurial ideas into work students can own.

## A statewide platform for student leadership

The [2026 OSBA Capital Conference](https://conference.ohioschoolboards.org/2026), held November 15–17 in Columbus, brought school district teams and education experts together to exchange ideas for Ohio’s public schools. For us, that meant speaking directly with people who shape the opportunities available to students across their districts.

Mason’s superintendent, **Dr. Cooper**, and our **school board president** attended our session. Their presence reflected a central part of our story: student ownership grows stronger when school leaders invest in it.

## Explaining how a student-led incubator works

I presented Mason Venture Studio as a fully student-led business incubator supported by our school. The focus was the practical structure behind that idea: how students take responsibility, how ventures develop, and how school support helps make the experience possible.

As a founding partner and Director of Programs & Operations, my work has included shaping the program, coordinating mentors, supporting student founders, and helping ideas move toward execution. At OSBA, I could connect those responsibilities to a larger question for educators: **what becomes possible when students help build the programs they participate in?**

Our team explained Mason’s programs, how they work together, and how student voice and leadership create real-world career experiences. Entrepreneurship became a concrete example of students practicing initiative, decision-making, collaboration, and accountability through work that matters to them.

## From a presentation to interest in visiting

The response made the experience especially meaningful. School leaders expressed enthusiasm for what we had built and interest in coming to Mason to see the programs in action.

That interest was an important step beyond the session itself. We were sharing a working example that other educators wanted to understand more closely. A conversation about our own experience had become a conversation about what student-led entrepreneurship could look like elsewhere.

## What this milestone means

Presenting at OSBA gave Mason Venture Studio a statewide audience and gave me the responsibility of representing the students, educators, and mentors behind it. I was proud to explain our work alongside peers who are also building meaningful opportunities at Mason.

The experience strengthened my belief that students can contribute to conversations about how education should evolve. We can bring ideas, show the work behind them, and help school leaders imagine what comes next.

**For Mason Venture Studio, this was a milestone in taking a student-built program beyond our own school—and inviting others to see its potential.**
