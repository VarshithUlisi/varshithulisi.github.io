## Starting with a question

MindMate began with my interest in access to mental wellness resources. Building the project meant learning about product design, artificial intelligence, user experience, and business strategy while trying to solve specific problems.

At first, revisiting an approach could feel like losing progress. Over time, I became more willing to step back when doing so could make the work more useful.

## Listening beyond my own assumptions

Conversations with mental health professionals were important because they brought experience I did not have. Their feedback pushed me to consider whether a feature belonged in the solution, not only whether it worked technically.

That distinction changed the way I approached product development. I needed to ask whether I understood the problem accurately and whether I was listening to the right people.

## What progress means now

I still enjoy the process of building, but I think more carefully about the responsibility that comes with something people might use. Effort does not guarantee an immediate result. Sometimes the useful next step is to ask what went wrong and try a different approach.

This is an account of learning and development. It does not claim validated clinical outcomes, a completed pilot, or a verified user total.
