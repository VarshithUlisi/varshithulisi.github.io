From June 7–11, 2026, I represented **MindMate Health** at the Junior Achievement Future Bound finals in Boston. We had been selected from a field of **900 teams**, and arriving at Boston University made the scale of that journey feel real.

MindMate was recognized as a **Junior Achievement Future Bound finalist** and nominated for the **Chick-fil-A Community Impact Award**. I was proud of the recognition, but what stayed with me most was the feeling that the project had moved into a much larger conversation.

## The national stage

The local competition in Cincinnati had given MindMate its national qualification. Boston was the next test: could I explain the problem, the product, and the long-term vision to people encountering the work for the first time?

At the exhibit, MindMate became more than a screen or a pitch deck. I spoke with business professionals, local-government professionals, and other students about why gaps in mental-wellness support matter, how the platform was being designed, and what responsible growth would require. Their questions pushed me to explain the idea more clearly and think more seriously about the path ahead.

## Finding my people

Future Bound was also an opportunity to meet students who were serious about building, competing, and creating impact. Being surrounded by high-achieving peers made the experience energizing rather than intimidating. We arrived with different ideas, but shared the willingness to take those ideas seriously enough to put them in front of other people.

That community mattered. It reminded me that ambitious work becomes stronger when it is challenged by people who understand the effort behind it.

## Recognition—and responsibility

The finalist recognition and Community Impact Award nomination gave MindMate credibility in the room. More importantly, they created conversations. People did not only congratulate the project; they asked where it could go, who it could serve, and what it would take to build it responsibly.

I left Boston proud, grateful, and more aware of the responsibility behind the idea. Recognition can open a door, but it does not build what comes next. That part still requires listening, iteration, careful decisions, and work.

## The first step in MindMate’s next chapter

Future Bound did not feel like the end of a competition. It felt like the first meaningful step in a much longer journey.

The experience gave MindMate a stronger foundation: a national platform, feedback from experienced professionals, recognition for its community-impact potential, and a clearer sense of what the project could become. The goal is not simply to collect awards. It is to keep improving the work until it can create meaningful, responsible value for the people it is designed to support.

Boston showed me that the idea could earn attention. What comes next is making sure that attention becomes momentum.
