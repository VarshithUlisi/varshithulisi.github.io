## A place to begin

On January 6, 2026, we launched Uplift Academy Ohio’s inaugural NextGen Coders pilot at Mason Public Library. Thirty elementary school students joined a free, 10-week bootcamp that ran through March 17, with twice-weekly sessions in coding and creative problem solving.

The idea was simple: give younger students a chance to become creators, with people nearby to help them get started. A public library and a team of high school volunteers gave that idea a practical home.

## Learning by making

Our curriculum used Code.org alongside hands-on activities. Students explored digital citizenship, internet safety, algorithms, and sequencing before experimenting with loops, debugging, and logical reasoning. They applied those foundations to artwork, animations, interactive stories, and games.

The goal was to leave room for questions and experimentation. A project that did not work immediately was a chance to investigate, ask for help, and try again.

![A student demonstrating a space-themed digital project with a mentor beside the lectern](/images/uplift-2026/cohort-03-1600.webp)

## Students teaching students

High school volunteers prepared lessons, led instruction, supported individual learners, and coordinated the program. Younger participants had mentors they could turn to; volunteers took responsibility for helping someone else learn.

The cohort reported **600+ student learning hours** and **450+ volunteer service hours** across **30 learners**. These are distinct measures of participation and service, rather than a claim of measured long-term learning gains.

## Sharing what they built

The final community showcase brought together **60+ attendees**, including students, families, volunteers, and supporters. Students demonstrated their original projects and explained the work behind them.

![A student and mentor discussing an animated Scratch project](/images/uplift-2026/cohort-04-1600.webp)

For me, this first cohort mattered because it put our model into practice: free access, structured learning, student mentorship, and a chance for children to share something they had made.

## The next chapter

The pilot laid the groundwork for NextGen Innovation Camp, which ran **July 18–August 8, 2026** at MidPointe Library and Mason Public Library. That summer, students connected coding with design thinking and global challenges.
