On February 21, 2026, I presented **MindMate Health** at Junior Achievement of OKI Partners’ local Social Innovation Challenge at the Cincinnati Museum Center. I finished **second locally** and officially became a **national qualifier**.

The plaque made the scale of that milestone tangible: national qualification recognized a finish in the **top 5% of all entries**.

## Bringing MindMate into the room

MindMate began with a question about access to mental-wellness resources. At the challenge, that idea had to become something another person could understand, test, and question: a working product experience, a clear problem statement, research, and a visual presentation that connected the pieces.

I demonstrated the platform, walked judges through the thinking behind it, and answered questions about the people it was designed to support. The experience forced me to move between product, technology, user experience, and business strategy without losing sight of the human problem that started the project.

## The moment it became real

Placing second was meaningful because it reflected the work behind the presentation. Becoming a national qualifier meant the project had earned the opportunity to keep moving.

It also changed how I thought about pitching. A strong presentation is not just a polished explanation of an idea. It is a live test of whether the problem is clear, whether the product choices make sense, and whether I can respond thoughtfully when someone sees the work differently than I do.

## What I carried forward

This event sits at the intersection of several parts of my story: building MindMate, learning to present technical work clearly, listening to feedback, and finding the confidence to put an unfinished idea in front of experienced people.

The result mattered. The larger lesson was that progress comes from being willing to show the work, hear the difficult questions, and return to the product with a sharper understanding of what it needs to become.
